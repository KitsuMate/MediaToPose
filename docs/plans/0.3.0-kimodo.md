# Media to Pose 0.3.0: Kimodo text to motion and in-betweening

Part of the 0.3.0 milestone (`docs/plans/0.3.0.md`), on the same branch and
under the same ground rules. This document is the complete plan for the
Kimodo integration and replaces Track B of the milestone plan.

Using Kimodo as a capture clean-up module is **not** part of 0.3.0. It is
kept in `docs/plans/learned-correction.md` and builds on what this plan
delivers: the SOMA conversion, the sampler, the guide warp and the apply
path.

## What users get

1. **Text to motion.** Type "A person walks forward, stops and waves" and
   get keyed motion on any mapped humanoid rig.
2. **In-betweening from blocking.**
   - Key a few poses: the full body, or only hands, feet or the root.
   - Generate the motion between them, with or without a prompt.
   - The keyed poses stay exactly as they were.
3. **Extend and bridge.**
   - Generate after existing animation, or between two pieces of it.
   - The neighbouring animation is the context, so the transition is
     continuous.
4. **Replace a section.** Clear a range and regenerate it from the poses on
   either side.
5. **Follow a path.** A curve object becomes the root path.
6. **Several prompts on one timeline.** Timeline markers, or a list of
   segments, give one prompt per stretch.
7. **Variations.** Generate N seeds side by side as NLA tracks, then keep
   one.

Text is optional for 2–5. Without a prompt only the 588 MB denoiser is
needed, not the 5.8 GB text encoder.

**Out of scope:**
- capture clean-up (see above);
- fingers, face and eyes (SOMA's model skeleton has none);
- awareness of objects or terrain;
- several characters at once;
- non-humanoid rigs;
- a 3D ghost preview, since no GPU overlay exists yet (candidates on NLA
  tracks are the preview in 0.3.0);
- prompt-adherence scoring (see Later).

## The model, as far as it shapes the design

From `nv-tlabs/kimodo@1aece8c`, `nvidia/Kimodo-SOMA-RP-v1.1`, the tech
report, and byte-range reads of the KitsuMate graphs.

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Denoiser (`KitsuMate/Kimodo-SOMA-RP-v1.1-ONNX`) | One step per call. Batch fixed at 3: text branch, constraint branch, unconditional branch. T is 2–300 frames at 30 fps. fp32 1.17 GB, fp16 588 MB, int8 306 MB (int8 weights, fp16 compute) |
| Inputs | `motion`, `observed_motion` `[3,T,369]`; `motion_valid` bool `[3,T]`; `constraint_mask` bool `[3,T,369]`; `text_embedding` `[3,50,4096]`; `timestep` int64 `[3]`, **the 0–999 diffusion step** (it indexes the sinusoidal table directly); `first_heading_angle` `[3]` |
| Representation (369) | `0:3` smooth root x and z, pelvis y · `3:5` heading cos, sin · `5:95` 30×3 joint positions minus smooth root xz · `95:275` 30×6D global rotations (columns 0 and 1) · `275:365` 30×3 velocities · `365:369` contacts (left heel, left toe, right heel, right toe; above 0.5 means contact) |
| Normalisation | `(x − mean) / sqrt(std² + 1e-5)` with `concat(global_root[5], body[364])` from upstream `stats/motion/*.npy` (float64). The `local_root` statistics live inside the graph |
| Axes | Y up, +Z forward, metres. Character left is +X. Rest pose is a T-pose. Heading θ = atan2(d_z, −d_x) with d = RightLeg − LeftLeg |
| Skeleton `somaskel30` | Hips; Spine1, Spine2, Chest; Neck1, Neck2, Head; Jaw, eyes; per side Shoulder, Arm, ForeArm, Hand, HandThumbEnd, HandMiddleEnd; per side Leg (the thigh), Shin, Foot, ToeBase. Rest offsets come from upstream `joints.p`, a torch pickle that is converted once |
| Sampler | DDIM, eta 0, cosine schedule over 1000 steps. Subsampled steps `ut[i] = round(i·999/(N−1))`. Separated CFG: `u + w_t·(text − u) + w_c·(constraint − u)`, weights 2.0 and 2.0. Upstream default is 100 steps; KitsuMate validated 25. Noise is one draw of `[T,369]` in normalised space |
| Constraints (in-distribution) | Up to about 20 sparse keyframes per type: full-body positions (all 30 joints, no rotations); hands or feet subsets (position plus base rotation; any subset of LeftHand, RightHand, LeftFoot, RightFoot, Hips); root 2D at sparse waypoints or a dense path, with optional heading. Every joint constraint also fixes root xz, pelvis y and heading at that frame. Foot-contact constraints were trained but are not exposed upstream. About 10 % of training had no constraints, and empty text is supported |
| Not trained | Arbitrary joint subsets beyond hands and feet; dense full-body or end-effector tracks |
| Windows | At most 10 s per prompt. Continuation uses the last 5 frames of the previous window as full-body and end-effector guides, re-canonicalises to the origin with heading from the hips, and cross-fades the overlap |
| Text encoder (`KitsuMate/Llama-3-LLM2Vec-MNTP-Supervised-ONNX`) | int4 (MatMulNBits, block 128), CPU, 5.8 GB. Inputs `input_ids`, `attention_mask`, `pooling_mask` int64 `[1,64]`; output one pooled `[1,4096]` vector. That vector becomes row 0 of the 50 text tokens; the other rows and the other branches are zero |
| Prompt | Upstream sanitises the text, prefixes `!@#$%^&*()`, wraps it in the Llama-3 user template, and pools over `prompt<|eot_id|>`. The tokenizer files match McGill's repository byte for byte |
| Licences | Denoiser: NVIDIA Open Model License (redistribution needs the licence and a NOTICE). Encoder: Meta Llama 3 Community License and AUP ("Built with Meta Llama 3" must be shown), plus MIT for LLM2Vec. Neither may be bundled in a GPL extension, so both are downloaded on the user's request |
| Limits upstream | Realistic single-person motion only. Flat ground at y = 0 (the model tends to sit low). Conflicting constraints get ignored. Feet skate and constraints are hit only roughly without post-processing. Prompts work best as "A person …" with one or two actions |

The open questions from the first draft are settled:
- the timestep is the 0–999 value;
- the `local_root` statistics are in the graph;
- the host owns CFG, DDIM, constraint compilation, decoding, windowing and
  clean-up.

## How it fits the code base

**Data flow:**

```
main thread            worker thread                    main thread
───────────            ─────────────                    ───────────
read guides,      →    encode prompt (cache)       →    SOMA → rig poses
context, path          plan windows                     root placement
→ GenerateRequest      per window: compile guides,      guide warp
  (frozen, core        DDIM 25–100 steps (cancel        contacts → grounding
   types only)         between steps), decode, stitch   write keys (transaction)
                       → GeneratedMotion (core)         → GenerateResult
```

**Modules,** following the layer rules (`core` is NumPy and the standard
library only; `bpy` from `rig`/`retarget` upwards):

|---|---|---|

| `core/motion/kimodo/guides.py` | core | `GuideSet` types and the compilation to `observed_motion` and `constraint_mask` |
| `core/motion/kimodo/sampler.py` | core | schedule, DDIM, CFG, `iter_sample(denoise_fn, …)` |

## Phases

### K0. Reference, assets and spike (`.workspace/kimodo/`; no add-on code)

1. **Upstream reference.**
   - A `.workspace` venv runs upstream Kimodo fp32 on CPU.
   - Record `tests/fixtures/kimodo/reference_60f.npz` (fixed noise, fixed
     embedding, one full-body guide, one hands guide and a root path):
     the normalised input, the per-step outputs for steps 0, 12 and 24, and
     the decoded joints.
   - Record `reference_embeddings.npz`: bf16 embeddings for 20 prompts, and
     reference token ids for 30 strings, including non-ASCII ones.
   - These are small, contain no pickle, and record their source in
     `fixtures.toml`.
2. **Host assets.**
   - Convert the upstream stats to `stats.npz` (float32 and float64 kept).
   - Convert `joints.p` (read once, offline, in the venv) to
     `somaskel30.json`: names, parents and offsets.
   - These go wherever Decision 1 says. The skeleton constants are small
     enough to commit in `core/motion/soma.py` as well, with the Apache-2.0
     attribution.
3. **Runtime spike** at T = 60, 150 and 300, with 25, 50 and 100 steps:
   - fp16 on WebGPU (ORT 1.27): does every node run on the GPU, including
     bool masks and dynamic reshapes? What are wall time and peak VRAM?
   - fp32 and int8 on CPU: wall time and peak RAM.
   - LLM2Vec int4 on CPU: load time, encode time, peak RAM, and cosine
     against bf16 over the 20 prompts. Check left padding against the
     graph's cumsum position ids.
4. **Quality spike.** A NumPy sampler prototype against the reference
   (cosine at least 0.9999 per step for fp32). Then 10 prompts × 3 seeds at
   25, 50 and 100 steps, rendered as sheets, to choose the default step
   count.
5. **Tokenizer.** Confirm that the `tokenizers` abi3 wheels install as
   extension wheels on Windows, Linux and macOS arm64 and reproduce the
   reference ids. Otherwise prototype a pure Python BPE: it needs a
   Unicode-class pre-tokenizer, because Python's `re` has no `\p{L}`.

The outcome goes into this plan's Outcome section: device per variant,
default steps, measured times and RAM. Every later phase uses these numbers.

### K1. Representation and sampler (`core/motion/kimodo/`, NumPy)

- **`rep.py`**
  - the slices as named constants;
  - `normalize` / `denormalize`;
  - `rot6d_to_matrix` (Gram–Schmidt) and `matrix_to_rot6d`;
  - `decode(features)` returns `KimodoFrames`: `[T,30,3,3]` global
    rotations, `[T,30,3]` positions, `[T,3]` root, `[T]` heading and
    `[T,4]` contacts;
  - `hip_heading(positions)`.
  - No velocity integration: positions are absolute.
- **`guides.py`** has frozen dataclasses with a `frame` (window index):
  - `FullBodyGuide(positions[30,3])`;
  - `EffectorGuide(effectors: frozenset[{LEFT_HAND, RIGHT_HAND, LEFT_FOOT, RIGHT_FOOT, HIPS}], positions[30,3], rotations[30,3,3])`;
  - `RootGuide(xz, heading | None)`.

  `compile_guides(guides, T, stats)` writes the same channels upstream
  writes:
  - full body writes all 30 positions, smooth-root xz, pelvis y and
    heading;
  - an effector writes the chain positions, the base 6D rotation, root xz,
    pelvis y and heading;
  - a root guide writes xz, plus heading if given.

  Where two guides hit the same frame and channels, the first wins. The
  smooth root of a pose guide is its pelvis xz, as upstream does when none
  is given. The 3D-smooth-root bug upstream (taking x, y instead of x, z)
  is not copied. `canonicalise(guides, origin_xz)` translates guides so the
  window starts at the origin.
- **`sampler.py`**
  - `schedule(steps)` returns `ut` and ᾱ, in float64 on the host;
  - `iter_sample(denoise_fn, noise, observed, mask, valid, text, heading, steps, w_text, w_guide)`
    yields `Progress` once per step;
  - `denoise_fn` takes the three-branch batch and returns
    `predicted_clean_motion`. It is injected, so tests and the stub backend
    need no ONNX;
  - noise comes from `np.random.Generator(PCG64(seed))`. Seeds are
    reproducible per device and variant, but do not match upstream torch
    noise; the parity fixture stores its noise for that reason.
- **`windows.py`.** `plan_windows(range_frames_30fps, segments, guides)`
  returns windows of at most 300 frames.
  - Each window lies within one segment, so prompts never mix.
  - A segment longer than 10 s splits into windows with a `WINDOWED`
    warning.
  - Windows after the first carry 5 transition frames from the previous
    window's decoded tail: full body plus all four effectors, as upstream.
  - Each window is re-canonicalised (origin at the first transition frame,
    `first_heading_angle` from its hips) and translated back afterwards.
  - The 5-frame overlap is cross-faded linearly, with rotations by slerp.
- **`prompt.py`.**
  - Upstream sanitising, except that `capitalize()` lowercasing is not
    copied ("NYC" stays "NYC"; whether it changes the embedding is
    checked in K0).
  - Empty text → no encoder and zero embeddings.
  - The template string, the pooling-mask length, and truncation at 64
    tokens with `PROMPT_TRUNCATED`.
- **`core/motion/retime.py`.** Map scene frames to 30 fps indices (guides:
  nearest index; collisions → `GUIDE_COLLISION`). Resample generated
  frames to integer scene frames: slerp for rotations, lerp for positions,
  nearest for contacts.

### K2. Model backends (`backends/kimodo/`, `backends/llm2vec/`)

- **Install.** Both install through `PinnedBundle` (as RoHM), with
  commit-pinned revisions and a manifest pinned by SHA-256.
  `install_pinned_bundle` gains three things, as separate commits shared
  with 0.3.0 Track A:
  - disk-space checks from SAM's `_check_bundle_storage`;
  - reuse of already verified files, so a 5.8 GB download resumes;
  - honouring `bpy.app.online_access`, passed in as a flag from the
    operator or API, with the error `ONLINE_ACCESS_DISABLED`.
- **Kimodo variants:**
  - `FP16` for WebGPU and CoreML, `FP32` or `INT8` for CPU, depending on
    K0;
  - `AUTO` picks by device;
  - one variant is installed at a time, as for SAM.

  The bundle is the ONNX file plus `stats.npz`, `somaskel30.json`,
  `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`.
- **Sessions.**
  - `KimodoSession` holds one ORT session. `select_execution_provider` is
    reused (WebGPU, CoreML, CPU). `GRAPH_OPTIMIZATION_LEVEL` is set to all.
  - Input dtypes are read from the session, so fp16 and fp32 share the
    code.
  - `denoise(motion, observed, mask, valid, text, timestep, heading)` →
    `np.ndarray`.
  - A GPU failure at runtime falls back to CPU in AUTO mode, as SAM does.
- **`LLM2VecEncoder`.**
  - Tokenizer (Decision 3), CPU session, and `encode(prompt) → [4096]`
    float32.
  - The session is created on demand and released straight after
    encoding.
  - **Resource check.** Before loading, `backends/resources.py` checks
    available RAM against the manifest's `min_ram_bytes` (about 8 GB),
    and raises `INSUFFICIENT_RESOURCES` with the numbers.
  - **Pinning.** A preference ("Keep text encoder loaded") holds it for
    batch work.
- **Prompt cache** (`core/cache/embeddings.py`). npz plus JSON with
  `schema_version`, keyed by
  `sha256(encoder revision | variant | template version | sanitised prompt)`,
  at 16 KB each, under the existing cache root with the existing size
  limit.
  - A cached prompt never loads the encoder.
  - `encode_prompt` warms the cache.
- **Lifecycle plumbing.**
  - `OperationKind` gains `KIMODO_*`, `LLM2VEC_*` and `GENERATE`.
    `active_session()` recognises `GENERATE`.
  - `api.model_status` gains entries for both, with variant, device,
    licence state and RAM need.
- **Licences.**
  - The install dialog and `models install` show a summary and the full
    `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`.
  - The user accepts per model revision. Acceptance is stored in the
    storage folder's marker JSON, with time and source (panel or CLI).
  - "Built with Meta Llama 3" appears in the Generate panel whenever
    prompts are enabled, in the README and in `docs/agents.md`.

### K3. Rig ↔ SOMA (`rig/soma.py`, `retarget/soma_pose.py`)

This is the core of the work. The retarget code's anatomical rotation is
relative to **the rig's own rest pose**, in a (right, forward, up), Z-up
frame. RoHM never aligns the rig's rest to the model's rest and gets away
with it only because of its 2° clamp. Kimodo needs real alignment.

- **Mapping.**

  | SOMA | Semantic |
  |---|---|
  | Hips | PELVIS |
  | Spine1, Spine2, Chest | SPINE_01, SPINE_02, CHEST |
  | Neck1, Neck2, Head | NECK, (none), HEAD |
  | Shoulder, Arm, ForeArm, Hand | SHOULDER, UPPER_ARM, LOWER_ARM, HAND |
  | Leg, Shin, Foot, ToeBase | UPPER_LEG, LOWER_LEG, FOOT, TOE |
  | HandThumbEnd, HandMiddleEnd | THUMB_03 and MIDDLE_03 tails when mapped; otherwise the hand's rest axes |
  | Jaw, eyes | not used |

  **Required roles:** the capture's required BODY roles (PELVIS, CHEST,
  HEAD, arms, legs, feet). This is less than RoHM's 22, because global
  rotations make missing intermediate bones harmless:
  - rig → SOMA: a SOMA joint with no rig bone gets its global rotation by
    slerp between the nearest mapped ancestor and descendant;
  - SOMA → rig: a rig bone takes the global rotation of its SOMA joint, so
    the missing joint's rotation is absorbed by the next mapped one.

  Readiness is checked up front from `rig_plan.semantic_bones`, never in
  the middle of a bake.
- **Axis change.**
  - Kimodo `(x, y, z)` → anatomical `(−x, z, y)`, a proper rotation
    (det +1). As a matrix `C`: a rotation maps by `C·G·Cᵀ`, a position by
    `C·p`.
  - The anatomical frame maps to armature space through `P` (the posed root
    basis) as today, and to world space through `matrix_world` for object
    roots.
  - K0 confirms the signs on a rendered T-pose.
- **Rest alignment** (computed once per rig plan and cached with it).
  - For each mapped SOMA joint, the rest rotation `O_j` takes the SOMA rest
    frame to the rig's rest frame, both in the anatomical frame.
  - **Swing:** shortest arc from the SOMA rest bone direction (joint →
    child) to the rig's (head → mapped child head).
  - **Twist:** align a secondary axis:
    - the hips line for pelvis, spine, neck and head;
    - the shoulder line for the clavicles;
    - the thumb–middle plane for the hands (or the bone roll when fingers
      are not mapped);
    - the foot–toe line for the feet;
    - the knee and elbow bend plane from the rest pose when it is bent,
      otherwise the anatomical forward.
- **SOMA → rig.** For each frame and mapped joint:
  `A_j = (C·G_j·Cᵀ) · O_j`. This feeds the existing conversion
  `target_delta = P @ A @ R⁻¹`, then `set_desired_rotation` with owner
  `GENERATE`.
  - **PELVIS gets a channel.** Today pelvis rotation is never written
    (`solve/body.py:40-41`).
- **Rig → SOMA** (for guides and context): `G_j = Cᵀ · (A_j · O_j⁻¹) · C`.
  - Then SOMA forward kinematics with SOMA's rest offsets gives positions
    on the model's own proportions. Rig joint positions are never copied,
    so limb-length differences cannot ask for unreachable poses.
  - Root height and path are scaled by the hip-height ratio: SOMA rest
    hip height against `_target_body_height`'s leg part.
- **Tests.**
  - Round trips: rig rest → SOMA → rig is identity within 1e-5, and a SOMA
    T-pose → rig → SOMA is identity.
  - Rigs: VRoid (T-pose rest, generic profile) and a generated Rigify rig
    (A-pose-like rest, IK and FK switches), built reproducibly by
    `tests/fixtures/rigs/build_rigify_fixture.py` from the bundled Rigify
    human metarig. The FaceFX ARP fixture has no body, so ARP body
    coverage is a Decision.

### K4. Guides, context and paths (`retarget/guides.py`)

All of this runs on the main thread, before any staging action exists. In
`NLA_STRIP` mode the staging action is empty, so guides must be read first.

- **Keys in scope.**
  - The armature's assigned action, **in its own slot**:
    `_action_fcurves` gains slot filtering (today it walks every slot).
  - Keyed frames inside `[first − context, last + context]`.
  - Bone channels: location, rotation and scale paths of mapped bones,
    their IK controls (from `limb_ownership`), the ROOT bone, and the
    object transform when ROOT is unmapped.
- **Classifying each keyed frame** by which channels are keyed there:
  - most mapped body chains keyed (torso, both arms, both legs; threshold
    in K0) → **full body**;
  - hand or foot chains, or their IK targets, keyed without the full body →
    **effector**, for those effectors only, plus HIPS when the pelvis is
    keyed;
  - only ROOT, pelvis or object location keyed → **root waypoint**, with
    heading if the rotation was keyed too;
  - only fingers, face or custom properties keyed → not a guide, and
    preserved.

  The panel and `guides` show this classification. Each guide can be
  switched off (stored in a `mtp_generate_ignored_frames` property on the
  armature).
- **Evaluating a guide.**
  1. `scene_context.set_frame(f)` and update with the original action
     attached.
  2. Read evaluated deform bones (`evaluated_semantic_bone`), so IK-keyed
     limbs are read correctly.
  3. Convert rig → SOMA as in K3. An effector guide still carries the whole
     evaluated pose, because upstream writes pelvis, hips and heading for
     every joint guide.
- **Context frames.** When animation exists right before `first` or right
  after `last` (within 5 frames at 30 fps), its evaluated poses become 5
  full-body plus effector transition guides, as in upstream continuation.
  This is what makes "extend" and "bridge" continuous. They are flagged
  `context` and never rewritten.
- **Density.**
  - More than 20 guides of one type in a window is thinned for the model,
    keeping the first, the last and evenly spaced ones between, with
    `GUIDE_TOO_DENSE`.
  - **All** keyed guides are still restored exactly by the warp (K5), so
    thinning costs nothing visible.
- **Plausibility.** If two guides need root speed above 8 m/s or a pose
  change above 720°/s, raise `GUIDE_UNREACHABLE` (a warning, not an error)
  and name the frames.
- **Paths.**
  - `path=<curve object>` samples the evaluated curve (world space, then
    the rig's ground plane) by arc length at constant speed over the range.
  - That gives a dense `RootGuide` track. The heading follows the tangent
    when `path_heading` is on.
  - Root waypoint keys inside the range keep their exact frames.
- **FK/IK switches.** `validate_limb_switch_interval` over the range. A
  switch that changes in the range is `InvalidRequest`, as for capture.
  Generation writes in whichever mode each limb is in, through the existing
  IK proposals.

### K5. Generation session and apply

- **`GenerateRequest`** (frozen) holds:
  - target identity;
  - frame range and segments (prompts with ranges);
  - the `GuideSet`;
  - seed, steps, `w_text` and `w_guide`;
  - candidates;
  - output mode and key settings;
  - device and variant;
  - `blend_frames`, `ground_height`;
  - the resolved rig plan signature.

  It is built by `GenerateSettings.from_rna`, or from API arguments, at
  the boundary.
- **`GenerateSession` phases** (`prepare`, `start`, `step(budget)`,
  `cancel`):
  1. **Prepare (main thread).** Readiness, guides, windows, a resource and
     model check, then a `GeneratePlan`; `--dry-run` stops here. Acquire
     `OperationKind.GENERATE`.
  2. **Infer (worker).** Embeddings come from the cache or the encoder,
     which is then unloaded. Then load the denoiser. For each candidate
     and each window: compile, sample (cancellable between steps), decode
     and stitch. The worker posts `("GENERATED", GeneratedMotion)`, or
     `CANCELLED` / `ERROR`, through the existing message pattern. Progress
     goes through `COORDINATOR.update` with an ETA from the K0 numbers.
  3. **Apply (main thread),** as a `step()` generator inside a
     `CaptureTransaction`:
     - retime to scene frames;
     - each frame becomes a `_BakedFrameSample` through `TargetPose` (SOMA
       → rig), `_prepare_ik_output` and `_commit_target_pose`;
     - root placement;
     - guide warp;
     - contacts and grounding;
     - write keys.
- **Root placement** (new `retarget/generated.py:_place_root`). The ROOT
  bone, or the object when ROOT is unmapped, takes the generated **smooth
  root** xz (scaled) and, when `root_yaw` is `FOLLOW_HEADING` (the
  default), the heading as yaw. The pelvis takes the remainder:
  - its height and sway become the PELVIS **location** relative to the
    root;
  - its rotation becomes a desired rotation.

  Rigs whose pelvis location is locked take the remainder on the root.
  Ground: Kimodo's y = 0 maps to `ground_height` (default: the rig's lowest
  rest foot point at the start frame, or 0). Upstream's 2 cm lift is
  replaced by grounding.
- **Guide warp** (`core/motion/guide_warp.py`, applied on baked channels).
  - At every keyed guide frame, compute each written channel's delta
    between the generated sample and the user's evaluated basis:
    quaternion delta for rotation, vector delta for location. Only
    channels the user keyed at that frame are warped.
  - Between two guides, interpolate the deltas (slerp, lerp). Outside, fade
    them to identity over `blend_frames` (default 8) with smoothstep.
  - Result: keyed frames match exactly, with a continuous first
    derivative. The size of each delta becomes `guide_warp_deg` and
    `guide_warp_m` in the result.
  - Warps above 30° raise `GUIDE_WARP_LARGE`, meaning the model ignored
    the guide: try more steps, a higher `w_guide`, or a closer prompt.
- **Contacts.**
  - Kimodo's contact channel (above 0.5, heel OR toe) feeds grounding:
    - `analyze_grounding` accepts precomputed contacts;
    - `_capture_grounding_frame` builds `ContactPoint`s without landmarks
      (image `(0, 0)`, confidence 1).
  - Then the existing root stabilisation and planted-foot repair run
    unchanged. This is the minimum change the survey found.
  - Feet constrained by an effector guide at a frame are not repaired
    there.
  - Smoothing (`_iter_temporal_channels`) is off by default for generated
    motion, which is already smooth. There is a setting for it.
- **Keys.**
  - `_iter_commit_baked_samples` gains a **protected frame set per data
    path**: user keys on guide and context frames are neither deleted nor
    rewritten, and the LINEAR re-pass skips them.
  - `REPLACE` and `NEW_ACTION` write generated keys on the remaining frames
    in the range. Keys outside the range and unmapped channels (fingers,
    face, custom properties) stay.
  - `NLA_STRIP` writes the full warped motion, guide frames included, on a
    new strip, because its staging action is empty.
  - Settings:
    - `key_spacing` (1 = every frame; N = every N frames);
    - `interpolation` (`LINEAR`; or `BEZIER` with auto-clamped handles,
      the default for spacing above 1, which skips the LINEAR re-pass).
- **Candidates.**
  - `candidates=N` samples N seeds (seed, seed + 1, …) in one worker run.
  - `keep=BEST` keeps the lowest `guide_error_m`, then the lowest foot
    sliding. `keep=ALL` puts each candidate on its own NLA track named
    `MTP Generate seed <s>`, all muted except the best.
  - `MTP_OT_keep_candidate` / `api.keep_candidate` bakes the chosen track
    into the action under the requested output mode and removes the
    others. This is the preview in 0.3.0.
- **`GenerateResult`** (schema 1) holds:
  - status, action, slot, output mode, NLA tracks;
  - the timeline range;
  - segments, windows, seed per candidate, steps and weights, variant and
    device;
  - guides used and thinned, with type, frame, `guide_error_m` (model
    output before the warp), `guide_warp_deg` and `guide_warp_m`;
  - `regenerated_frames`, `seconds` per phase, and warnings;
  - `recipe`, all resolved inputs, for `--recipe` replay (see the
    milestone's command line section).

  Warning codes: `PROMPT_TRUNCATED`, `WINDOWED`, `GUIDE_TOO_DENSE`,
  `GUIDE_COLLISION`, `GUIDE_UNREACHABLE`, `GUIDE_WARP_LARGE`,
  `CONTEXT_USED`, `FINGERS_KEPT`, `GENERATE_WARNING`.

### K0 (2026-10-19)

Machine: RTX 3070 Laptop (8 GB), 16 logical CPUs, 34 GB RAM, Windows 11.
**The machine was shared during the runs** (another Blender job used the
CPU, at about 56 % load), so CPU times are upper bounds and every absolute
number is indicative. Ratios between variants and step counts are the
reliable part; re-measure on an idle machine before ETA defaults are fixed.
The GPU was otherwise idle. Scripts and raw JSON are in `.workspace/kimodo/`
(`scripts/`, `spike/`, `review/`).

**Upstream reference.** Upstream Kimodo fp32 on CPU (torch 2.14) runs from a
`.workspace` venv. `kimodo` does not install as a package on Windows (a
dependency needs CMake), so the sources go on `PYTHONPATH` with the listed
dependencies. An instrumented copy of `_generate` matches upstream bit for
bit with injected noise. `ci/kimodo_reference.py` records three fixtures and
regenerates them byte for byte:

- `tests/fixtures/kimodo/reference_60f.npz` (2.2 MB). T = 60, 25 steps, CFG
  (2, 2). It holds:
  - PCG64 noise (seed 2024, equal to `sampler.initial_noise`);
  - the int4 embedding of "A person walks forward and waves.";
  - a dense root path (frames 0–29), a full-body guide (40) and a hands
    guide (59), all taken from an unconstrained sample;
  - the normalised observed motion (zero where unmasked) and the mask
    (184 bits);
  - the full per-step trace (float16), with float32 x_t and float16 raw
    branches at calls 0, 12 and 24;
  - the decoded output.

  No NVIDIA statistics or weights are stored.
- `reference_tokens.npz`: upstream ids for 30 strings, including
  non-ASCII, empty, and one of 90 tokens.
- `reference_embeddings.npz`: int4 embeddings for 20 prompts.
  `tests/unit/test_kimodo_reference.py` loads all three.

**Parity of the NumPy code on `feature/0.3.0`** against the fixture:
- `schedule(25)` gives the same timesteps (999, 957, …, 42, 0).
- Replaying the trace through `sampler.sample` reaches a final cosine of
  0.9999998 (the float16 storage limits it).
- `compile_guides` gives an identical mask; values agree to 2.7e-7 after
  upstream normalisation.
- `rep.decode` positions agree within 1.2e-6 m, rotations within 2.2e-7,
  and contacts are identical. This also confirms the `soma.py` rest offsets.

**Timestep.** Confirmed: the ONNX `timestep` is the 0–999 diffusion step
`round(i·999/(N−1))`. Fed that value, the step-0 branch cosine against
upstream is 1.0000000 (fp32). Fed the loop index instead, it is 0.017.

**Denoiser runtime** (ORT 1.27 `onnxruntime-webgpu` wheel as bundled; full
sampling loop through `sampler.sample`; seconds):

| Variant / device | T=60: 25 / 50 / 100 | T=150: 25 / 50 / 100 | T=300: 25 / 50 / 100 | Peak RAM | VRAM | 25-step cosine vs upstream |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fp16 WebGPU | 1.6 / 2.7 / 5.8 | 2.4 / 4.8 / 10.0 | 3.9 / 8.1 / 16.1 | 0.74 GB | +0.62–0.67 GB | 0.99702 |
| fp32 WebGPU | 1.6 / 3.4 / 6.9 | 3.3 / 6.4 / 13.1 | 4.8 / 10.7 / 20.6 | 1.30 GB | +1.20–1.31 GB | 1.0000000 |
| int8 WebGPU | 1.6 / 3.5 / 7.5 | 2.9 / 6.0 / 11.6 | 5.2 / 9.4 / 18.6 | 0.47 GB | +0.47–0.51 GB | 0.99682 |
| fp32 CPU | 14.6 / 31.6 / 61.4 | 29.6 / 53.9 / 109 | 50.5 / 98.8 / 185 | 1.30 GB | – | 1.0000000 |
| int8 CPU | 17.9 / 35.4 / 73.5 | 35.2 / 67.0 / 134 | 83.4 / 160 / 280 | 0.69 GB | – | 0.99805 |
| fp16 CPU | 15.2 / – / – | 26.6 / – / – | 58.2 / – / – | 1.73 GB | – | 0.99999 |

- Session creation takes 1.6–2.9 s. The first call at a new T costs 0.1–0.5 s
  of shader compilation on WebGPU.
- Every variant on WebGPU places 294 of 1,463 nodes (int8: 294 of 1,743) on
  CPU. These are only int64 shape arithmetic (Gather, Unsqueeze, Concat,
  Shape for the dynamic reshapes) and the `motion_valid` ReduceSum. All
  compute runs on WebGPU, including the bool-mask `Where`/`Not`, `ScatterND`,
  `Atan`, attention, LayerNorm and MatMul.
- Every output was finite at every length.
- fp16 single-step branch cosines are 0.9996–0.9998. The 25-step drift to
  0.997 is trajectory divergence, as KitsuMate reported, not an error.

**Variant per device (recommendation):**
- **WebGPU: fp16.** It is the fastest, about 1.2–1.3× faster than fp32 at
  T ≥ 150, with half the VRAM. int8 saves only 0.15 GB of VRAM and is not
  faster.
- **CPU: fp32.** int8 is slower on CPU (fp16 compute is emulated) and less
  exact. It only halves RAM, to 0.7 GB, so offer it as a low-RAM option.
- fp16 on CPU is no faster and uses more RAM.
- CoreML was not measured (no Mac).

**Text encoder** (int4 LLM2Vec ONNX, CPU; the bf16 encoder was not
downloaded, at the maintainer's choice):
- Session load takes 8.3 s (warm file cache); an encode takes 2.4–3.0 s
  (median 2.7 s).
- Peak working set is 4.7 GB, and 4.3 GB is resident after load. Use about
  6 GB as `min_ram_bytes`, with headroom.
- Left padding to 64 with the graph's cumsum position ids is correct:
  changing the pad id leaves the embedding bit-identical.
- Cosine against bf16 was **not measured**. The integration test can only
  check int4 against `reference_embeddings.npz` until a bf16 reference
  exists.
- Lowercasing matters a little: "A person walks to NYC." against "… nyc."
  has cosine 0.990. Not copying `capitalize()` therefore changes the
  embedding slightly. Keep the case-preserving plan, and note that it is not
  bit-equal to upstream.
- Upstream encodes without padding (batch size 1) and with `max_length`
  512. The 64-token graph therefore truncates anything over 58 prompt tokens
  (the template takes 6).

**Tokenizer (Decision 3).**
- `tokenizers` 0.22.2 (`cp39-abi3`) reproduces all 30 reference sequences
  and pooled lengths on win_amd64 (Python 3.13) and manylinux x86_64 (WSL,
  Python 3.14), loaded from unpacked wheels as Blender does.
- The macOS arm64 wheel was not run (no Mac). It is the same Rust crate
  version, but needs a check on a Mac or in CI.
- Recommendation: bundle the wheels.
- The ids are `<|begin_of_text|>` + `<|start_header_id|>user<|end_header_id|>\n\n`
  + prompt + `<|eot_id|>`, with pad `<|end_of_text|>`. The pooled tokens
  are the encoding of `prompt<|eot_id|>`, taken from the end.

**Quality and default steps (Decision 4).** The sweep was 10 prompts × 3
seeds × 25/50/100 steps, at T = 150 with fp32 on WebGPU, text only.
Skeleton sheets are in `.workspace/kimodo/review/`.

| Steps | Foot sliding (m/s) | Jitter (m/s²) | Lowest foot (m) | Mean joint distance to the 100-step result (m) | Time (s, fp32 WebGPU) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 0.033 | 3.1 | 0.013 | 0.50 | 2.9 |
| 50 | 0.033 | 3.8 | 0.013 | 0.25 | 5.9 |
| 100 | 0.033 | 4.0 | 0.013 | 0 | 11.8 |

- No step count shows artifacts the others lack. Feet, ground contact and
  prompt adherence look alike on the sheets.
- The seed fixes the action. More steps add amplitude and detail: longer
  strides and stumbles, livelier arms. At 25 steps motion is slightly
  smoother and smaller.
- The distance to 100 steps is large only for locomotion prompts, where
  stride phase and travel drift. For in-place actions it is 0.03–0.16 m.
- Recommendation:
  - **default 50 steps**, under 5 s for a 5 s clip on WebGPU fp16;
  - **"Fast" = 25 steps**, also the default on CPU, where 50 steps of a
    10 s window take about 100 s;
  - **"Quality" = 100 steps**.
- Prompts that need more than 5 s ("sits down … and stands up again") do
  not finish within 150 frames at any step count. That argues for a longer
  default window, or a hint in the docs.

**Host assets.** The upstream statistics are float64 (`body` 364,
`global_root` 5, `local_root` 4). A `stats.npz` with float32 and float64
copies is staged in `.workspace/kimodo/assets/`, waiting on Decision 1
(upload needs approval). The `somaskel30` offsets in `core/motion/soma.py`
already reproduce upstream FK, so no JSON is needed for the add-on itself.
